## Project overview

Automator (working product name Relay) is a multi-tenant SaaS for AI sales employees. A business configures an AI employee with its own approved knowledge, tests it privately, and will then use it to respond to customer enquiries and support its sales process.

The product is **in active development**. This case study describes the foundation that is built and verified today, and is explicit about what is still to come.

## Business challenge

Sales enquiries often arrive when nobody is available to answer them, and slow responses lose customers. AI can help, but a business cannot hand customer conversations to a model without control over:

- what the AI is allowed to know and say,
- who in the business can change its configuration,
- how much it is allowed to cost, and
- how its data is kept separate from every other customer's.

## Operational context

Each customer is a business with its own team, currency, locale and time zone. Within a business, owners and admins configure the AI employee, while other members may only need to read its setup. Across all businesses, a platform operator needs oversight of tenants, accounts and usage without bypassing tenant boundaries.

## My role and responsibilities

- Usage limits, audit trails, the platform control center and automated tests.

## Solution approach

The product is built in **verified vertical slices**. A module counts as complete only when its interface, backend, persistence, authorization and tests are all in place, and anything less is tracked as partial.

The AI is constrained by design. Each AI employee answers from **approved business knowledge**, and owners and admins test it in a **private test chat** before anything is customer-facing. Model responses use **structured outputs**, so the application validates what comes back instead of trusting free text.

## Key implementation decisions

- **Client claims never grant access.** Slugs, cookies and client-selected organization IDs never establish authority. Access is always resolved from the database.
- **Transactions for tenant creation.** An organization, its owner membership and its creation audit event are committed together, or not at all.
- **Usage budgets before cost.** Daily request and token ceilings are reserved before a model call, and duplicate submissions cannot call the provider twice.
- **A separate platform role.** Platform administration is a singleton grant, independent of organization roles, and every platform read checks it.

## Challenges and trade-offs

The main trade-off is speed against trust. Building tenant isolation, auditing and usage controls before public chat slows the first customer-facing release, but these are the guarantees a business needs before letting an AI talk to its customers, and they are much harder to add afterwards.
